# Life timeline validation (embedded tables)

A check of the "Life timeline" numbers ([life-expectancy.md](life-expectancy.md)) against official sources,
done on 2026-10-20 with the real tables embedded in
`crates/treasury-core/data/longevity/wpp2024.zip` (UN, World Population Prospects 2024,
medium variant, and IBGE, Tábuas Completas de Mortalidade 2024), generated by
`scripts/longevity-data.py`. The tests are in `crates/treasury-core/src/longevity/validation.rs`
and run with the rest of the core (`cargo test -p treasury-core longevity::validation`); the table
below comes from `print_the_validation_table` (`-- --nocapture`).

The app's method (the same in the script and in the core): S(a) = 1, S(x+1) = S(x) × (1 − q(x)) and
e(a) = 0.5 + Σ S(x) for x > a. Period = the rates of a single year; cohort (generation) = the diagonal
of the rates, year by year, from 1950 to 2100 (after 2100, it repeats 2100).

## 1. Brazil: period 2024, UN × IBGE

| Sex | e0 UN (app) | e0 IBGE | Difference | e60 UN (app) | e60 IBGE | Difference |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Both | 76.03 | 76.61 | −0.58 | 21.90 | 22.59 | −0.69 |
| Men | 72.96 | 73.31 | −0.35 | 20.39 | 20.80 | −0.41 |
| Women | 79.15 | 79.87 | −0.72 | 23.26 | 24.15 | −0.89 |

- **Tolerance: 1.0 year** (`WPP_VS_IBGE`). The UN estimates Brazil's rates with its own model,
  from several sources, and IBGE publishes the official table; the UN is lower in all
  rows, more so for women and at 60. This is the expected difference from
  §4.5 ("the UN rates are modeled and may differ a little from IBGE's"), and it is why
  the app shows IBGE alongside as a reference in Brazil.
- The app's calculation reproduces the UN's own expectancy: Brazil's e0 2024 (both) is 76.023
  in the UN file and 76.03 in the app (the difference comes from the 0.5 year in place of the UN's `ax` for
  those who die in the year and from the 16-bit rounding of qx).
- **The embedded IBGE reference** reproduces the published figures (tolerance 0.06, rounding
  only): 76.6 / 73.3 / 79.9 at birth, 22.6 / 20.8 / 24.2 at 60, 8.3 (men) and
  9.5 (women) at 80.

## 2. United Kingdom: cohort and period, UN × ONS

| Case | UN (app) | ONS | Difference | Tolerance |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Boys born in 2023, by cohort | 88.01 | 86.7 | +1.31 | 1.5 |
| Girls born in 2023, by cohort | 90.79 | 90.0 | +0.79 | 1.5 |
| Men, period | 79.54 (2024) | 78.8 (2021–2023) | +0.74 | 1.0 |
| Women, period | 83.32 (2024) | 82.8 (2021–2023) | +0.52 | 1.0 |

- The ONS uses the 2022-based projection and the three-year period 2021–2023 (still with the effect
  of COVID); the UN uses the 2024 projections and the year 2024. The assumptions about future
  improvement also differ (the UN's gives more years to the male cohort: +1.3). Tolerances:
  **1.5 years for cohort and 1.0 for period** (`ONS_COHORT_TOLERANCE`, `ONS_PERIOD_TOLERANCE`).

## 4. Properties (all 63 countries, three sexes)

`properties_hold_in_every_country`, no tolerance:

- survival never rises with age (today's ages 0, 18, 41, 65, 90, 100 and 105);
- the median is always above today's age;
- the chance that at least one of two is alive is never lower than that of each one;
- **cohort ≥ period**: the cohort expectancy for those born from 2024 to 2026 is never
  below the 2024 period one, in any country and sex.

The test `embedded_tables_cover_the_catalog` also checks that every country in the catalog has a table and
that the stored cohort expectancy (calculated by the script, without rounding qx) matches the one
calculated by the core from `qx.bin` to within 0.05 year.

## 6. How to update

1. Download the new files (`python3 -I scripts/longevity-data.py --urls` shows which).
2. `python3 -I scripts/longevity-data.py UN_FOLDER --ibge IBGE_FOLDER` (the printed summary
   already compares with IBGE and the ONS).
3. `cargo test -p treasury-core longevity` and, with `--nocapture`, update the tables above; if
   a difference exceeds the tolerance, investigate before raising the tolerance.
